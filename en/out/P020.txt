Some people say that the vine in John chapter 15 is the answer to Ezekiel chapter 15. Is it fine to say that?
@@6572@@
Yes, you can see a connection between the two places, but do not set them equal. John 15:5 says: “I am the vine. You are the branches. He who remains in me and I in him bears much fruit, for apart from me you can do nothing.” Jesus is the true vine, and he is also the one true branch joined to the Father. This points in the same direction as Ezekiel 15: without the connection, there is no fruit. The difference is that Ezekiel speaks as a warning, while John speaks as an invitation. So you can use them this way: first let Ezekiel tear down the illusion that “I am useful on my own,” then let John bring you into the rest of “remaining in him.” The order must not be reversed, and do not blend the two passages into one.
@@6573@@
The first question: we are used to measuring ourselves by “whether we are useful.” From childhood we are asked, “What place did you get in the exam?” When we grow up we are asked, “What is your job, and how much do you earn?” When we come to church it becomes, “Which department do you serve in?” After a while we tie our worth to our results. Once we retire, lose a job, fall sick, or can no longer help, we feel like a useless branch. The question in 15:3 is meant to untie that rope. You can begin this way: this week, find an older person or a sick person whom you feel “cannot help with anything,” and simply sit beside him. Do not ask what he can do. Just say to him, “You are here, and I am glad.”
@@6574@@
The second question: we are used to thinking that “having a title is enough.” We say, I am the descendant of so-and-so, I am a member of such-and-such a church, I have been baptized, I am a deacon, I am an elder. All of these are good, but they are like a name plate on the trunk, not fruit on the branch. The people of chapter 15 said, “We have the temple, we have Jerusalem.” You can check yourself this way: this month, was there even one time when I truly showed patience, joy, and peace toward a person, rather than only attending the meetings? If not, do not rush to do more things. First go back to the Lord, and quietly stay with him for a while.
@@6575@@
In Chinese churches there is a lot of service. How can I tell whether I am “bearing fruit” or “burning firewood”?
@@6576@@
You can ask three questions. First, after I finish this thing, do I love the Lord more and love people more, or am I more tired and more anxious? Fruit carries life; firewood carries weariness. Second, in whose name am I doing it? If it is to be seen and praised, that is wood; if it flows out naturally because I am connected to him, that is fruit. Third, do I have quiet time just to remain in the Lord’s presence, not asking for anything and not proving anything? If not, service easily turns into burning yourself up. You do not have to stop all your service because of this. You can start with one thing: this week, set aside a period of time to simply remain in his presence. If you are already so tired that it affects your emotions and health, please talk with a pastor or a trusted companion, and also seek professional help when necessary.

VII. Living Out 321: Remaining on the Vine
@@6578@@
Jesus is my Role Model. He said that he is the true vine, and that he lives completely in the Father: he does nothing on his own, but only what he sees the Father doing. He is the branch that was most fully connected, and he also walked into the greatest fire. We read chapter 15 not to learn how to be more useful than others, but to learn how he remained in the Father’s love.
@@6579@@
The Bible is my Standard. The question in 15:2 is asked of every one of us: do I measure my worth by his word, or by other people’s eyes? The standard is not on my report card; it is in what God says. He says that the worth of a vine is in its fruit, so I no longer use wood to prove myself.
@@6580@@
The Holy Spirit is my Guide. Fruit is not squeezed out; it is borne by the Spirit on a branch that is connected. Each day, take a quiet moment and ask: Holy Spirit, where today am I trying again to squeeze out fruit by my own strength? Bring me back into you.
@@6581@@
Let Jesus be King. What this chapter asks me to hand over is the sovereignty of “I must be useful”: my worth, my titles, my service record. To let Jesus be King is to let him decide whether I am a branch, instead of my proving myself with wood. Selflessness is not being nothing; it is no longer proving myself with wood, and asking only: am I remaining in him?
@@6582@@
Let Jesus receive all the glory. If we bear a little fruit, let us not say, “This is mine.” The fruit of the vine has never been the glory of the branch; it is the glory of the one who planted, the one who watered, and the one who waited. Even the fruit is to be given back to him.
@@6583@@
To build God’s system. A group that belongs to God is not a pile of useful timber; it is a vineyard joined to the vine. Life first, then relationships, then ministry; fruit comes out of life, not out of the strength of the organization. In such a group, no one is overlooked because he cannot help, and no one is lifted up because he is very capable.
@@6584@@
How do “Let Jesus be King” and “I must be useful” fit together?
@@6585@@
They do not conflict; they simply come in a different order. To let Jesus be King does not mean we do nothing; it means he decides what I do and why I do it. “I must be useful” is often the voice of self: I want to be needed, I want to be seen. A person who lets Jesus be King still works and still serves, but the voice in his heart is different: I do it because I am connected to him; if I cannot do it, I am still his branch. You can practice this way: every morning say three sentences to Jesus: what I most want to prove today; what I most fear being seen as useless in today; today I place these two things in your hands. In time you will find that service becomes lighter, because you are no longer carrying it alone.
@@6586@@
The proud type says, “I have ability and experience; I am the person the church needs.” The passage says to him: its wood cannot even hold a single pin. Pride is leaving one’s position as a man, seizing God’s authority, and wanting to prove oneself by oneself. Returning to one’s position is to admit: I am the branch, and he is the vine. You do not need to say it all at once. First say one sentence to God: “Lord, I will no longer use my wood to prove myself.”
@@6587@@
The fearful type says, “If I am no longer useful, will I be thrown away?” The fire in chapter 15 is frightening, but please notice that the direction of the whole book is for people to know Yahweh, not to make them tremble every day. The fearful type needs to learn to replace “I am afraid of being thrown away” with “I am connected to him.” You can begin with the smallest thing: tonight before you sleep, say one sentence to God: “I am your branch.”
@@6588@@
The shame type says, “I can’t do anything well; I am a useless branch.” Shame says “you are useless,” and the gospel says “you were planted.” Your worth is not what you have done, but who planted you. The shame type does not need to fix himself first; it is enough today to remain in his presence for a little while.
@@6589@@
The wounded type says, “I have given so much to the church, yet I was left out in the cold and told I was useless. I don’t want to bear fruit anymore.” God does not blame you for being hurt. You can honestly tell him, “I am tired. I am in pain.” Those who accompany such a person should not rush to say “you have to let go,” and should not rush to use this chapter to explain his suffering. Just say, “I am here, and I will walk with you.”

Chapter 15 is very short, yet it has a complete arc. At the beginning there is one branch, and God asks: what strength does it have? In the middle there is fire, and God says: it does not even qualify as timber. At the end there is desolation, and God says: then you will know that I am Yahweh. From one branch to a desolate land, this is a chapter that makes us fall silent.
@@6601@@
It does not give us a beautiful ending, because God wants us first to see the truth: on our own, we are nothing; joined to him, we have life. But it also leaves a crack in the door: later a shepherd will come, a new heart will be given, and dry bones will stand up. The true vine came among us in Jesus, and he also walked into a greater fire and bore the judgment for us.
@@6602@@
So please remember the three main points of today’s lesson: the worth of a vine is in its fruit, the fruit is in the connection, and those who are connected need not fear. The focus of this lesson is not how useful you are, not how others see you, and not how desolate things look right now, but Jesus, who planted you and waits for you.
