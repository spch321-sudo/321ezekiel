<p><strong>What does “אִדָּרֵשׁ” mean?</strong></p><ul><li><strong>“I will let them inquire of me”</strong> (permissive sense): God is willing to be asked.</li><li><strong>“I will be sought”</strong> (passive sense): God’s grace comes about because people seek.</li></ul><p>This Niphal verb is mostly permissive in the prophets: God is willing to be sought.</p><blockquote><p><strong>The best understanding: God stands ready to be inquired of.</strong> The promise comes first, and the invitation to ask is there too.</p></blockquote>
@@15937@@
<p><strong>“They must ask me about this”: even for what he has promised, God invites people to ask.</strong></p><p>Since he has already resolved to do it, why should people still ask? The verb in the original means “I am willing to be inquired of” (<em>’iddarésh</em>). Prayer is not persuading God to change his mind. It is an invitation to step into the work he is already doing.</p><p>At the very end the picture changes to a flock of sheep. In Chapter 34 he said he would be the shepherd himself and look for the scattered sheep. Here the flock has come back, and there are so many of them, like the sheep offered in Jerusalem at the feasts. A city at feast time is packed shoulder to shoulder, and this is the hope of this city.</p><p>Please notice that this is the last picture of Israel in this chapter: the ruins filled with a crowd. Not an army, but a flock being shepherded.</p><p>Verse 37 says “moreover” (<em>‘od</em>). It is the last thing in this chapter, and also the start of something else. Next, in Chapter 37, the prophet will be brought to a plain covered with bones, to watch how this people are brought out of death.</p><p>What he has already promised, he still invites you to ask for. This is the seat he gives you, and you may sit down in it.</p>

@@16279@@
Verse 1 says: “You, son of man, prophesy to the mountains of Israel, and say, ‘You mountains of Israel, hear Yahweh’s word.’ ” Please notice that God says “hear” to a mountain. In the last chapter Mount Seir was silenced. In this chapter another mountain is woken up. Mountains cannot talk, but God told the prophet to speak to them, as if this ruined land were also his child, with ears that can hear comfort.
@@16280@@
Verses 2 and 3 spell out the mountains’ trouble: the enemy said, “Aha! The ancient high places are ours in possession!” They have made you desolate and swallowed you up on every side, and you are taken up in the lips of talkers, with an evil report among the people. Verse 4 goes over the picture in detail: mountains and hills, watercourses and valleys, desolate wastes and forsaken cities, which have become a prey and derision to the rest of the nations all around. As you read this, you will find that it is not only the land that is mocked, but the people who live on it. When the land is called “beyond saving,” the people are called “beyond saving” too.
@@16281@@
But in verses 5, 6, and 7, God’s tone changes. He says, “in the fire of my jealousy I have spoken.” He burns with zeal for this land that is laughed at. Verse 8 continues: “You, mountains of Israel, you shall shoot out your branches, and yield your fruit to my people Israel; for they are at hand to come.” Verse 9 is the warmest sentence in the whole passage: “For, behold, I am for you, and I will come to you, and you will be tilled and sown.” Please notice “I am for you.” The idea in the original is: I am turned toward you, I face you. God’s face has turned toward that wasteland.
@@16282@@
Verses 10 to 12 are a string of “I will”: I will multiply men on you, the cities will be inhabited, the waste places will be built, man and animal will increase, and they will walk on you. The last sentence of verse 12 is very gentle: “you will never again bereave them of their children.” Verses 13 and 14 say that people said to this land, “You are a devourer of men, and have been a bereaver of your nation.” God answers: you shall devour men no more, and not bereave your nation any more. We should read this very slowly, because the words “bereave of children” are a real wound for some people. This passage is clearing the land’s name. It is not pointing a finger at anyone. What God does here is take away the name “You are a devourer of men.”
@@16283@@
A part of my own relationships or body is like that wasteland, and I feel “there is no saving it.” What can this passage say to me?
@@16284@@
The first thing it says to you is verse 9: “Behold, I am for you.” Notice that God did not begin by saying “you must try harder.” He first said “I am for you.” Wasteland cannot till itself. Someone has to come. Second, verse 11 says God will do better for you than at your beginnings. This is his promise, not your achievement. Third, please don’t use this passage to rush yourself to get better quickly. If you are in the middle of grief, serious illness, or a long season of exhaustion, this passage only means: you are not abandoned, and he faces you. Today you can say one sentence to him: Lord, I am that wasteland; please turn toward me.
@@16285@@
II. They Profaned My Holy Name (36:16–21)
@@16286@@
Verse 16 is a new beginning again: “Moreover the word of Yahweh came to me.” This time God pulls the camera back to the past and explains why these mountains became desolate. Verse 17 says: “when the house of Israel lived in their own land, they defiled it by their ways and by their deeds. Their way before me was as the uncleanness of a woman in her impurity.” This line must be read carefully. It is a picture from the Old Testament rites of cleansing, and it is saying “what is unclean needs to be cleansed.” It is not shaming women, and it is not treating a bodily process as sin. No one should feel ashamed of their body because of this picture.
@@16287@@
Verse 18 says that God poured out his wrath on them for the blood they had shed in the land, and for defiling it with their idols. Verse 19 says: I scattered them among the nations. Please notice that God links two things: shedding blood, and idols. One is harm done to people, the other is turning away from God. These two are really two branches of the same tree: people who leave God end up hurting people, and people who hurt others have often already set God aside.
@@16288@@
But verse 20 is the turning point of the whole chapter: “When they came to the nations where they went, they profaned my holy name; in that men said of them, ‘These are Yahweh’s people, and have left his land.’ ” This sentence is heavy. People look at a group of exiles and say: This is Yahweh’s people? How did they end up like this? God’s name was dragged into their disgrace. A brother told me that at his company he did something dishonest, and a colleague said privately, “You Christians are like this too.” He said that the hardest thing at that moment was not being looked down on, but knowing that he had dirtied the Lord’s name.
@@16289@@
The words of verse 21 can make the eyes burn: “But I had respect for my holy name.” In the original, “had respect” carries the sense of pity, of not being able to bear it. God is not coldly watching his name be dirtied. He is grieved. We often think God is concerned about his face, but what this says is that God and his name are bound together. When his name is dirtied, it is another way of saying that the people he loves are being dirtied.
@@16290@@
God says “I had respect for my holy name.” It sounds as if God is very concerned about himself. Isn’t that a bit selfish?
@@16291@@
That is an honest question. We need to distinguish carefully. If a person cares about his own name, it may be pride. But God is Creator and Redeemer, and his name is his faithfulness and his love. For his name to be profaned means that when the world sees his people, they wrongly think he is unreliable and unloving. So “for my holy name” does not say that he does not love people. It says that the source of his love is his own faithfulness, not people’s performance. This is an even greater comfort to us: whether you deserve it is not the reason he acts. Who he is, is the reason. You can say to him: Lord, I don’t deserve it, but you are faithful. Please act for the sake of your own name.
@@16292@@
III. Light from the Original Language: Name, Heart, Spirit (36:20, 26, 27)
@@16293@@
There are three Hebrew words in this chapter worth a close look. The first is “name,” שֵׁם (shem). Verses 20 and 21 say “my holy name,” in the original שֵׁם קָדְשִׁי, literally “my holy name.” In Hebrew, a “name” is not just what someone is called. It is the whole person: who he is, whether he can be trusted, how he treats people. So when God says “I had respect for my name,” he means: I care about how people come to know me. What you say today at the office, at home, and online is the first calling card by which others come to know him.
@@16294@@
The second is “heart,” לֵב (lev). Verse 26 says, “I will also give you a new heart,” in the original לֵב חָדָשׁ. It goes on: “I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you a heart of flesh.” The “stony heart” is לֵב הָאֶבֶן, and the “heart of flesh” is לֵב הַבָּשָׂר. In the Hebrew way of thinking, the heart is not only feelings, but the place of thought, will, and decision. A stony heart is not the heart of a wicked person, but a heart that no longer responds to God’s voice. A heart of flesh is a heart that can hurt, can be touched, and can respond. Please notice that the text does not say “you must move the stone away.” It says “I will take it away.” The stone is to be moved by God.
@@16295@@
The third is “spirit,” רוּחַ (ruakh). In verse 26 the new spirit is רוּחַ חֲדָשָׁה. In verse 27, “I will put my Spirit within you” is, in the original, רוּחִי, my Spirit. This word is wind, breath, and spirit all at once. Please notice the difference between the two verses. Verse 26 is a new spirit. Verse 27 is his own Spirit. New life is not an upgraded version of ourselves, but God putting his own breath within us. This is what 11:19 foretold, and it is the rebirth that John chapter 3 speaks of, the scene the New Testament opens for us.
@@16296@@
Join the three words together, and you have the journey of this chapter: God has respect for his name (shem), so he sets his hand to replace a heart (lev), and puts his own Spirit (ruakh) inside. The name is the cause, the heart is the means, and the Spirit is the power. These three words can be carried home in thirty seconds. Today, am I letting his name be known? Is there a stone in my heart that needs him to take it away with his own hand? Am I leaning on my own strength rather than on his breath?
@@16297@@
Ezekiel 18:31 says “make yourselves a new heart,” while 36:26 says God will “give” a new heart. So who does it?
@@16298@@
Both places are Scripture, and there is no contradiction. Chapter 18 is an appeal: people must turn and respond. Chapter 36 is a promise: God gives, God works. They are two sides of the same thing. The human responsibility is to stop stiffening the neck and be willing to hand over the stone. God’s work is to truly take away the stony heart and put the new spirit in. We often remember only one side. If we only remember “people must try hard,” we become worn out. If we only remember “God will just do it,” we become passive. A healthier way to say it is: I hold out my hand, and he puts the heart in. Today you can do just one thing: say to him, I am willing, please come and do it.
@@16299@@
IV. Not for Your Sake, but for My Holy Name (36:22–38)
@@16300@@
Verse 22 is the heaviest sentence of grace in the whole book: “Therefore tell the house of Israel, ‘The Lord Yahweh says: “I don’t do this for your sake, house of Israel, but for my holy name.” ’ ” At first hearing this sounds a little cold. But please think: if God did this for our sake, then the day we were no longer good, his grace would stop. But he says it is for his name, and his name does not change, so his grace will not stop. This is a sentence that puts the weak at ease: the root of grace is not in my performance.
@@16301@@
From verse 24 comes a string of actions: “I will take you from among the nations and gather you out of all the countries, and will bring you into your own land.” Verse 25: “I will sprinkle clean water on you, and you will be clean.” Verse 26: a new heart, a new spirit, the stony heart taken away, a heart of flesh given. Verse 27: “I will put my Spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes.” Please notice the subject. It is all “I.” I take, I sprinkle, I give a heart, I put in the Spirit. People are only the ones who are taken, cleansed, renewed, and led.
@@16302@@
About “bring you into your own land,” different interpreters read it differently: some read it as the return after the exile, some as a spiritual return, and some as a fulfilment still to come. These notes set out the different views side by side. We do not make any one of them a condition of faith, still less name any modern nation, and we do not draw up a timetable. We hold on first to the clearest point: God will bring a scattered people back into his presence. As for “sprinkle clean water,” churches also differ in how they understand baptism and cleansing. We do not make any ritual a condition of salvation either. The point is that the cleansing is something he does.
@@16303@@
Verse 28 is the language of covenant: “You will be my people, and I will be your God.” Verses 29 and 30 speak of grain and the fruit of the trees and the field, so that you receive no more the reproach of famine among the nations. This is a covenant picture, not an insurance policy for life. People in poverty who read this need not feel their faith is lacking. What it means is: he does not want his people to be mocked by others as abandoned.
@@16304@@
Verses 31 and 32 are very interesting: “Then you will remember your evil ways, and you will loathe yourselves in your own sight for your iniquities and for your abominations.” Verse 32 says: “be ashamed and confounded for your ways.” Please notice the order: this “loathing of yourselves” comes after the new heart is given, not before. It is within God’s grace that a person has the courage to look at their own evil. What is loathed is the evil ways and abominations, not the person. Verses 33 to 35 are the high point of this chapter: the waste places are rebuilt, and the passersby say, “This land that was desolate has become like the garden of Eden.” The garden of Eden is the original picture of people living with God. Verse 36 says: “I, Yahweh, have spoken it, and I will do it.” Finally, in verses 37 and 38, God says that they must ask him about this, and he will do it for them. He is willing to be inquired of.
@@16305@@
When I read “new heart,” I still feel cold and numb, feeling nothing at all. Does that mean my stony heart has not been taken away yet?
@@16306@@
Please don’t condemn yourself like that. The “stony heart” is a description, not a charge. It describes a heart that no longer responds to God’s voice. Numbness and depression are often the natural reactions of the body and soul of someone who has been wounded, and they do not necessarily mean unbelief. The point of 36:26 is that God says: I will give you. He is the subject. You do not have to feel something first before he can renew you. You can start with just one small thing: say one sentence to him every day: Lord, I can’t feel anything, but I am willing. If numbness and weakness have lasted a long time, please also find a pastor or a professional to walk with you.
@@16307@@
The first deviation is to preach “I don’t do this for your sake” (36:22) as “God does not care about people.” What the text means is that the root of grace is his name and his faithfulness, not people’s performance. We should help the congregation be more sure of his faithfulness, not feel that they are not loved.
@@16308@@
The second deviation is to preach verse 26 as “if you try harder to feel, you will get a new heart.” The new heart is given by God, not made by people. The human turning in 18:31 and God’s renewing in 36:26 must be read together. We should help the congregation lean on God more, not become more worn out.
@@16309@@
The third deviation is to say to those who are numb, depressed, or afraid to hope: your stony heart has not yet been taken away. A stony heart is a description, not a charge. A person in anguish should come away from the message invited, not condemned. Walking with them matters more than correcting them.
@@16310@@
The fourth deviation is to use the “uncleanness of a woman in her impurity” (36:17) to shame women, or to treat a bodily process as sin, and also to use the “bereaver” of verses 13 and 14 to blame those who have lost someone. This passage is a picture from the Old Testament rites of cleansing, and it is clearing the land’s name. If a hearer feels ashamed or blamed because of it, that way of preaching must stop.
@@16311@@
The fifth deviation is to preach the abundant harvest of verses 29 and 30 as “believe in the Lord and get rich,” to preach the “inquired of” in verse 37 as “the more you pray, the more God does what I want,” or to preach the new heart as pressing wounded people with “you must forgive, you must go back.” He is willing to be inquired of, not talked into anything; the new heart is given by God, not pressed on people. We should help the congregation love the Lord more, not treat prayer as a tool.
@@16312@@
When I hear “bring you into your own land,” can I take it as a prophecy of the present world situation?
@@16313@@
These notes do not read it that way. Interpreters differ on the “return” in verse 24: some read it as the return after the exile, some as a spiritual return, and some as a fulfilment still to come. We do not make any one of them a condition of faith, we do not name any modern nation, and we do not draw up timetables or count days. What this chapter really wants us to hold on to is that God will bring scattered people back into his presence, and that it is he who does it, not we who calculate. If someone uses this verse to make you afraid, please go back first to verse 22: he does this for the sake of his name. The result of reading Scripture should be to love the Lord more and to be more patient, not more anxious.
@@16314@@
The first question: we are used to “turning over a new leaf,” but not very used to “being renewed.” Chinese culture values ceaseless self-improvement. When someone has a bad habit, our first reaction is: make up your mind and get rid of it. We make many resolutions, and fail many times, and then we say: I’m just this kind of person. Chapter 36 says that a stony heart is not moved away by resolutions; he takes it away. You can begin like this: find one place where you have made many resolutions and failed, and say to God: I can’t do this one, please come. Then do one small thing today, not to prove anything, but to cooperate.
@@16315@@
The second question: we are used to “reporting the good and hiding the bad,” and we dare not admit that we are a piece of wasteland. In Chinese churches, when people meet we often say “very good, very peaceful,” but after going home, relationships, health, and moods each have their own weeds. Ezekiel 36:9 says that God says to the wasteland: “Behold, I am for you.” You can begin like this: find someone you trust and say your wasteland in one sentence: In one area, I am like a piece of land overgrown with weeds. No defending, no dressing it up. You will find that saying it out loud is not shameful. It lets the one who helps you come in.
@@16316@@
Chinese churches value “testimony” very much. If I say I am still in the wasteland, will it look as if I have no faith?
@@16317@@
No. Testimony in the Bible has never been only testimony of “already succeeded.” It also includes testimony that “he is still at work.” Verses 34 and 35 say that it is passersby who see the wasteland become Eden, and then speak. That is the testimony of the “result.” But before that there was a long stretch of desolation, and he was already facing that land. If you say honestly, “I am still in the wasteland, but I believe he faces me,” that itself is faith. You can say: I am not well yet, but I know he is helping me. When others hear a testimony like that, they are more willing to speak of their own wasteland.
@@16318@@
VII. Living Out 321: A New Heart, a New Spirit, a New Life
@@16319@@
Jesus is my Role Model. At the Last Supper he raised the cup and said: “This cup is the new covenant in my blood.” He is the Lord who truly brought the new heart and the new spirit into the world, and the Lord who paid the price on the cross for our stony hearts. Those who follow him learn his way: not relying on their own strength, but relying on the Father’s Spirit.
@@16320@@
The Bible is my Standard. We read chapter 36 not to memorize one promise, but to let this promise change how I see myself: I am not abandoned wasteland, I am the land he faces. Verses 22 and 26 must be read together: the root of grace is his name, and the way of grace is giving a heart and giving a spirit. Let this sentence be the reason I no longer say “I’m just this kind of person.”
@@16321@@
The Holy Spirit is my Guide. Verse 27 says: “I will put my Spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes.” This foretells the indwelling of the Holy Spirit in the New Testament. Obedience is not me gritting my teeth to do it, but him enabling me within. Each day be still for a moment and ask one sentence: Lord, what small thing is your Spirit leading me to do today?
@@16322@@
Let Jesus be King. Selflessness is putting down the pride of “I will fix myself,” and also putting down the self-verdict of “I am just beyond saving.” To let Jesus be King is to hand over the stony heart and hand over the decision: you take it away, you give, you reign as King.
@@16323@@
Let Jesus receive all the glory. Verse 22 says it very clearly: not for your sake, but for my holy name. If my life changes even a little today, I will not say “I finally changed.” All the glory goes to the Lord who takes away the stony heart and gives a heart of flesh. When glory goes to him, we are free.
@@16324@@
To build God’s system. God’s system is a group of renewed people encouraging one another: wasteland will still become Eden. First life, then relationships, then ministry: first let him replace the heart, then talk about relationships with others, and only last ministry. If a small group can say to one another, “I feel nothing today, but I am willing,” and all wait together, that is a small God’s system: a garden that he is planting on abandoned land.
@@16325@@
What does “a new heart and a new spirit” have to do with “Let Jesus be King”?
@@16326@@
The connection is direct. A new heart means my heart has a new master. A new spirit means I have a new source of strength. To let Jesus be King is to admit that the key to this heart is in his hand, and I no longer make myself my own king. You can practice it like this: when you get up in the morning, say one sentence to him: “Lord, today this heart is yours, and this decision is yours.” Then notice where during the day you take the key back. When you take it back, don’t blame yourself; just say it again. After a while you will find that obedience is no longer gritting your teeth, but being led.
@@16327@@
The proud type says: “I can change myself. I don’t need anyone to help me get a new heart.” The text says to him: I don’t do this for your sake, but for my holy name. The proud person leaves his position and wants to make a new heart for himself. To return to one’s position is to admit: the new heart is given by him. You don’t have to say it all at once. First say one sentence to God: “Lord, I can’t do this one. Please come.”
@@16328@@
The fearful type says: “If he really sets his hand to this, will he take my whole self apart?” The text says: I will cleanse you, I will give you a heart of flesh. His hand is a hand of love, and what he takes away is the stone, not you. The fearful type needs to learn to change “afraid of being touched” into “being renewed by hands that love me.” You can begin with the smallest thing: put one small habit you are gripping tightly into his hands.
@@16329@@
The shame type says: “It has been so many years, and no one can change me.” The text says to the wasteland: “Behold, I am for you.” Shame says “you are beyond saving.” The gospel says “you are the land he faces.” The shame type does not need to make himself good first. Today just say one sentence to him: “Lord, I am that wasteland. Please turn toward me.”
@@16330@@
The wounded type says: “I was hurt too deeply. My heart has hardened, and I don’t dare feel again.” God does not blame you for your hardness. It is the way you protect yourself. He says: I will give you a heart of flesh. This is his invitation, not pressure. A wounded person can slowly find someone trustworthy and tell your story. The one who accompanies should not hurry to say “you must forgive” or “you must open up,” but only say: “I’m here. Take your time.”
@@16331@@
First, spend three minutes every day praying: “Lord, I want a new heart. Please take away my stony heart and give me a heart of flesh.” Write down the date.
@@16332@@
Second, write down one place where you have made many resolutions and still cannot do it, and beside it write: “I can’t do this one. Please come.” And say whom you are willing to let remind you.
@@16333@@
Third, tell a companion about one piece of your “wasteland,” in one sentence, with no details. Ask him to pray for you, and say his name and the time.
@@16334@@
Fourth, this week do one very small thing that cooperates with his renewing: for example, apologize, tidy up, go to bed early, or read Scripture for ten minutes. Write down which day you did it.
@@16335@@
Fifth, send a sentence to a friend who feels “beyond saving”: “I see you, and I’m with you.” Don’t urge him to get better quickly, don’t press him to pull himself together, and say his name.
@@16336@@
I declare: he is the God who helps me, and he turns toward me.
@@16337@@
I declare: I will no longer say “I’m just this kind of person,” because the new heart is given by him.
@@16338@@
I declare: his Spirit lives within me and enables me to obey.
@@16339@@
I declare: all the glory goes to Jesus, because he has spoken it, and he will do it.
@@16340@@
The simplest thing is this: tonight, take a piece of paper and write down one piece of your “wasteland,” in just one sentence, for example: my temper, my sleep, my relationship with someone. Beside it write: “Lord, behold, I am wasteland. Please turn toward me.” Then put the paper by your bed and read it once before sleeping. If you are grieving, seriously ill, or in a long season of exhaustion, just doing this tonight is enough. You don’t need to do anything more. Bring this paper to the next meeting, and you don’t have to show it to anyone. This is your first hoe-stroke, between you and him. At the next small group, just say: This week I did one thing. No need to say what it was.
@@16341@@
Chapter 36 begins with a mountain and ends in a garden. It begins with God saying to the mountains of Israel: you must hear. In the middle are the profaning of his holy name and God’s respect for it. Then comes the new heart, the new spirit, and the stony heart taken away. At the end, the desolate land has now become like the garden of Eden, and the people are many like a flock. From wasteland to garden, from stone to a heart of flesh, this is a journey in which he does not give up.
@@16342@@
In this chapter, God wants us to see that the source of grace is his name and his faithfulness. God wants us to see that the new heart and the new spirit are given by him. And God wants us to see that desolate land and desolate people are all in his eyes. That God, in Jesus, came among us. He is the mediator of the new covenant, and the Lord who puts his Spirit within us.
@@16343@@
So please remember three things today: the mountains, the name, the new. The focus of this lesson is not how desolate you are, not how hard the stone is, and not how hard you try, but Jesus, the one who says “Behold, I am for you.”
@@16344@@
Lord, thank you. You act for the sake of your own name, and you turn toward us. Please forgive us for saying “beyond saving,” and forgive us for thinking we can make our own new hearts. Take away our stony hearts, give us hearts of flesh, and put your Spirit within us. We pray in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ. Amen.

“Our hope is lost.” Some words are not spoken with the mouth, but with the whole person. Anyone who has been sick for a long time, has waited for a long time, has wept for a long time, has a plain of bones inside. The exiles said this about themselves: our bones are dried up, our hope is lost, we are completely cut off. God did not correct them. He took the words just as they were, and then led the prophet into the middle of the bones and asked: Can these bones live? The answer of chapter 37 is not “you must have faith,” but “I will put my Spirit within you, and you will live.”
@@16372@@
Dry bones can live, not because people pull themselves together, but because God’s word and Spirit come. He makes them stand up, makes them one, and dwells with them.

God’s Spirit led the prophet into a plain full of dry bones and asked: Can these bones live? The prophet was commanded to prophesy to the bones and to the breath. The bones received sinews, flesh, and skin, the breath entered, and they stood up. These bones are the whole house of Israel, who said their hope was lost. Then two sticks became one stick in God’s hand, David became king, and God made a covenant of peace with them, with his sanctuary among them forever.

Grace in this chapter is all his verbs: I will open, I will bring, I will put. This is a promise not yet fulfilled, but already spoken. Even in the grave, he still calls them “my people.”

Final integration:
There was a plain full of bones.
Not fallen yesterday,
but long ago, and very dry.

God asked: Can these bones live?
The prophet said: You know.
He did not say they could, nor that they could not.
He simply handed the question back to him.

He spoke.
Bone joined to bone, with sinews, with flesh, with skin,
but there was still no breath.
He spoke again, to the wind.
Breath came in, and they stood up.

He said: My people.
Even inside the grave, nothing was changed.
He said: I will put my Spirit within you.

Two sticks became one,
one shepherd, one king,
and in the end, his dwelling place is among them.

This is selflessness.
Selflessness is not nothing; selflessness is that I speak, he breathes, and he reigns as King.

The definition of pride: leaving one’s position as a human being, grabbing God’s power, and wanting to take his place. The pride of chapter 37 is thinking the bones must be put together by me, and that unity must be brought about by me. The breath is not mine to manage. Speaking is my position.
