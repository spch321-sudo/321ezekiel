How do “Let Jesus be King” and “the king bows at the threshold” relate to leadership?
@@21155@@
If Jesus is King, then I am not the last king. This understanding lets everyone who leads put down two burdens: first, “I must be right about everything,” and second, “I must stand higher than others.” The king bowing at the threshold is the most honest position for a leader: I am also one who has come to worship. A concrete practice is this: before making an important decision, stop for thirty seconds and say to him, “This matter is yours; I am only keeping watch over it for you.” Then ask one more question: “Does this decision cause anyone to lose what they have received?” This question will make you go a little slower, but you will walk more steadily.

@@21157@@

Shame says, “You don’t deserve it”; the gospel says, “He opened the door.” The ashamed person does not need to get himself in order first. Today, walk in.

<p><strong>The temple of God is not only a place to be entered, but also the place from which life flows out.</strong></p><p>Ezekiel has been led all along to measure the temple, look at the temple, and hear the rules of the temple. Here the chapter suddenly stops, and what he sees is not walls, not gates, but water.</p><p>The water comes out from “beneath the threshold.” The threshold is where people step, the lowest point of the whole temple. The water of life does not come out from the throne; it comes out from under the feet.</p><p>There is one more detail: the water flows “eastward.” The temple faces east, and the water goes out from the east gate toward the wilderness. When the glory returned, it came from the east (43:2); when the water flows out, it also goes east. From whichever direction it came in, it goes out in that direction.</p><p>This water is not a flood. The verb in the original (מְפַכִּים) means a fine flow, dripping out little by little. But you will see that this fine water later grew into a river.</p><p>Landing: is there a place in you that has always been “taking in” but has not “flowed out”? God’s temple never meant to keep the water inside itself. He puts life into you so that it may flow out.</p>
