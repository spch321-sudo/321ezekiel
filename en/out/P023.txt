<p><strong>① “Make yourselves a new heart” and God giving a new heart (36:26)</strong></p><ul><li><strong>Human responsibility</strong>: the command here is real, and people are to do it</li><li><strong>God’s gift</strong>: in chapter 36 God promises “I will give you a new heart”</li><li><strong>Both together</strong>: the command lets people see their need, and the promise tells who supplies it</li></ul><p><strong>② וִחְיוּ</strong> is an imperative: “Live!” Not “you will live,” but an exit.</p><blockquote><p><strong>Best understanding: command and gift do not conflict.</strong> A command God gives is also a call he has prepared to supply.</p></blockquote>
@@7605@@
<p><strong>One command, with the heart of the whole chapter hidden in it.</strong></p><p>“Make yourselves a new heart and a new spirit.” It sounds like an impossible demand: how can a heart make itself? Many people feel pressed down when they read this.</p><p>First look at one thing: this command is not placed after rebuke, but among invitations. “Why will you die?” That is the tone of someone standing at a door calling out. It is not a judge passing sentence, it is a father calling: do not take that road.</p><p>Then look at the last verb: “live,” and it is an imperative: live! It is like a doctor saying to a patient: you can get up now.</p><p>Who gives the new heart and new spirit? In chapter 36, God will say: “I will give you a new heart.” The command here lets you see that you need it; the promise there tells you who supplies it.</p><p>The heaviest sentence of the whole chapter is “I have no pleasure in the death of the one who dies.” God is not forced by people into forgiving. He is, from the beginning, the one who wants you to live.</p><p>This word “live” is the last word of the whole chapter. From verse 9 on it has been spoken by God all the way to here. The whole chapter speaks, really, of this one word.</p><p>Landing: You may have a hundred reasons for leaving him. He asks only one thing: why? Come back.</p>
