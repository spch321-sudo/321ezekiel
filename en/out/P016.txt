I. Moving Baggage by Day, Digging Through the Wall by Night (12:1–7)
@@5375@@
Brothers and sisters, peace to you. Have you ever watched a neighbor move out? The day before, cardboard boxes begin to pile up at the door, one, two, five. The shoe cabinet is empty, and the curtains are taken down. You walk by and cannot help asking, “Are you moving?” They do not need to explain. That pile of boxes has already answered you. What God told the prophet to do in this chapter is exactly that: in broad daylight, pile up baggage at the door where the whole city can see it. He is not moving house. He is speaking. And what he uses is not his mouth, but a pile of baggage.
@@5376@@
In verse 2 God first gave a very heavy diagnosis: “Son of man, you dwell in the middle of the rebellious house, who have eyes to see, and don’t see, who have ears to hear, and don’t hear.” The prophet had heard this diagnosis many times. He spoke, and people did not listen. He spoke louder, and they listened even less. Have you had this experience? You say the same sentence to a person, a child, or a church for ten years. God’s response is interesting: he did not tell the prophet to speak louder. He told him to change the way, and speak with his body.
@@5377@@
Verses 3 to 6 give the instructions, one by one, very concretely: by day, in their sight, bring out your stuff, like stuff for moving into exile; in the evening, go out yourself, as those who go into exile go out; in their sight dig through the wall and carry the stuff out through it; in the dark, bear it on your shoulder and cover your face, so that you do not see the land. “By day” and “in their sight” are repeated several times: God wants to be seen. Please look again at the last sentence of verse 3: “It may be they will consider, though they are a rebellious house.” It may be. God knew their hearts were hard, yet he still left a “may be.” He did not stop speaking just because the other side would not listen.
@@5378@@
In verse 7 the prophet did just as he was commanded: he brought out his stuff by day, dug through the wall with his hand in the evening, and bore it on his shoulder in the dark. The text says it in one sentence, with no praise, no explanation, and no complaint. We can imagine that night: his hands covered in dirt, the neighbors watching from behind their windows, and no one coming out to help. This is a very lonely obedience. But please remember that this was a special charge God gave to the prophet. It is not telling us to go and dig through other people’s walls. What we are to learn is his willingness to be seen: God spoke, and he went and did it, and did not stop just because no one understood.
@@5379@@
I have spoken for many years and the other person still does not listen. Should I give up?
@@5380@@
The rebellion in verse 2 is a fact God himself saw. He did not tell the prophet to be quiet because of it, but to change his way. So you can ask yourself: do I have only one way of speaking? Sometimes what needs to change is not the content but the way: a meal, a short letter, a way of living that you do first yourself. The “may be” in verse 3 is worth carrying with you. You cannot guarantee that the other person will understand. You only need to guarantee that you are still there, and have not stopped loving him out of discouragement. Please also remember that you are only the one who passes the word on. It is God who makes people turn back. If you are tired, you may rest first, ask the brothers and sisters for help, and keep praying for him.
@@5381@@
II. The King, in the Dark (12:8–20)
@@5382@@
The next morning, God’s word came again. In verse 9 the house of Israel did ask: “What are you doing?” In verse 10 God said that this concerned the prince in Jerusalem and all the house of Israel around him. Verse 11 says: “I am your sign. As I have done, so will it be done to them. They will go into exile, into captivity.” So yesterday’s pile of baggage was packed for them. What the prophet was doing was the road they were about to walk.
@@5383@@
Verses 12 and 13 speak of the king: he will also carry his stuff out on his shoulder in the dark, dig through the wall, and cover his face so that he does not see the land. “I will also spread my net on him, and he will be taken in my snare.” This is a very heavy passage, and we will not paint the scene. Many scholars say it agrees with the later historical record. The text itself does not name anyone, and we do not need to add it. There is one very clear line here: verse 2 says they have eyes and do not see; by verse 13, a person really cannot see. Having eyes and not looking ends in not being able to see. It is not that God suddenly became cruel. A person kept on not looking, and in the end had no chance to look at all.
@@5384@@
In verses 14 and 15 God says that all who help him round about, he will scatter to every wind; when he scatters them, they will know that he is Yahweh. This sentence appears many times in the whole book. His purpose is not destruction, but that people would know him. Knowing him is the final exit of the whole book. Then comes verse 16, the gentlest sentence in this passage, hidden in the darkest place: “But I will leave a few men of them from the sword, from the famine, and from the pestilence.” A few are left. In the midst of judgment there is a list of those who are left. They are left not to live comfortably, but to declare: we were wrong, and he is real.
@@5385@@
God says “I will leave a few men of them.” Does that mean most people have been thrown away?
@@5386@@
The “leaving” in verse 16 is a sign that mercy is still kept in the middle of judgment. It is not counting who is thrown away and who is chosen. The text gives us no number and no list, and we do not need to add one. What you can see is his heart: he leaves people so that in the nations where they come they may declare, and people may know that he is Yahweh. That is to say, even survival is a charge, not a privilege. It is a place where you are asked to open your mouth and bear witness. If you have ever been left standing after a storm, you can ask yourself: to whom can I say how he treated me?
@@5387@@
In verses 17 to 20 God gave the prophet another sign: “Eat your bread with quaking, and drink your water with trembling and with fearfulness.” This time it is not about digging a wall, but about daily life. A meal, a cup of water, and a trembling hand. Verse 19 explains that this is said to the people of the land: they will eat their bread with fearfulness and drink their water in dismay, that the land may be desolate, because of the violence of all those who dwell in it. Please notice the point of the explanation: the land is made desolate because of violence. It is not saying that someone who cannot eat must be guilty. If you cannot eat or sleep well right now, please do not use this passage to judge yourself. Anxiety is real. You do not have to prove first that you did nothing wrong before you bring it to him.
@@5388@@
The end of verse 20: the cities that are inhabited will be laid waste, and the land will be a desolation; “Then you will know that I am Yahweh.” Again that sentence: you will know that I am Yahweh. At the end of this whole passage there is not a wasteland but knowing. However heavy the judgment is, the purpose of his speaking is still to bring people back to him. This is the voice the whole book keeps repeating. We must not read past it.
@@5389@@
I have been anxious and sleepless lately. Is “eating with quaking and drinking with trembling” God judging me?
@@5390@@
Do not read it that way. Verse 19 is very clear: it is said about a land made desolate by violence, and it describes a whole place losing its peace. It is not judging one anxious person. Your anxiety is real, and you do not need to prove you have no sin before you come to him. Before you sleep you can say one sentence to him: Lord, I am very unsettled, and I place it before you. Please also let someone you trust walk with you, and tell them about your sleep and your appetite. If it is already affecting your work and life, please see a doctor or find professional counseling. Seeing a doctor is not a lack of faith. It is taking care of the body God gave you.
@@5391@@
III. Days Prolonged, Visions Failed? (12:21–28)
@@5392@@
In the second half of the chapter the scene changes. Yahweh’s word came again: “Son of man, what is this proverb that you have in the land of Israel, saying, ‘The days are prolonged, and every vision fails’?” This was a popular saying, on everyone’s lips, like what we often say: “They have said so much for so many years, and nothing has happened.” Have you heard something like that? I have prayed for so long, and there is no movement. The church has said it so many times, and no one has changed. The prophet has spoken of judgment for so many years, and the city is still standing. After a while, people learn to use a saying to put God’s word away in a drawer.
@@5393@@
What is most powerful about this saying is that it sounds reasonable, because the days really have passed, and the thing really has not happened yet. We cannot say it is nonsense. The weariness of waiting is real. But God’s response is not to comfort them and tell them to wait a little longer. First he takes this saying away: “I will make this proverb to cease, and they will no more use it as a proverb in Israel.” Then he puts another sentence in its place: “The days are at hand, and the fulfillment of every vision.”
@@5394@@
Please notice that God gave no date and no calendar. He only replaced the word “prolonged” with “at hand.” We must be very careful not to read this passage as a basis for counting days. The text does not tell anyone to count. What this passage is putting to an end is two attitudes: one is denial, it will not happen anyway; the other is postponement, that is for later, and has nothing to do with me.
@@5395@@
In verse 27 the second saying appears: “The vision that he sees is for many days to come, and he prophesies of times that are far off.” This sentence is more humble than the first, and even sounds a little devout: I am not saying I do not believe, only that it is for later. Postponement is harder to notice than denial, and it makes sure a person never has a day of turning back. God’s answer is only one sentence, in verse 28: “None of my words will be deferred any more, but the word which I speak will be performed.” Verse 25 also says: what I speak will be performed, and will be no more deferred. This sentence is not a timetable. It is the signature of the God who speaks and it is done, left by his own hand.
@@5396@@
For the weary, this verse is a comfort. If you have grown tired in the waiting, God has not said that your faith is not enough. What he says is: my word is reliable, more reliable than your weariness. “Not yet” does not mean “never.” But for another kind of person, this sentence is a reminder: do not put his word in a very distant place, as if it had nothing to do with today. Listen today, take the first step today, and what he says will begin to be done from today. This reliability does not depend on our grasp. It depends on his own name. What you hold on to is not a date, but a God who keeps his word. That is enough.
@@5397@@
I have waited a long time and my prayers have had no answer. Is “will be no more deferred” rebuking me for not having enough faith?
@@5398@@
No. What chapter 12 rebukes are those who treat “not yet” as “never,” not the weary. God did not tell Israel not to be tired. He only took away that proverb they were using to block his word. So you can say honestly to him: Lord, I am very tired of waiting. This is not unbelief. It is coming to him with your weariness. At the same time, please do not comfort yourself by counting days, for his word is more reliable than your calculations. You can choose one small thing you can do today and turn waiting into a small daily obedience: read a verse, send a greeting, or just sit down and say to him: I am still here.

IV. Light from the Original: Sign, Proverb, Word (12:6, 22, 25)
@@5400@@
This chapter has three original words worth pausing on. The first is “sign,” in Hebrew מוֹפֵת (mofet). Verse 6 says: “for I have set you for a sign to the house of Israel.” This word is not an ordinary mark. It is a sign that people will look at and that carries meaning, and the prophet himself was the sign. God set the prophet as a sign, which means his whole person became a sermon. Turned into life: when people cannot take in words, they watch you. What do your family, colleagues, and neighbors see today? You do not need to become some great sign, but your daily life is already a silent sermon.
@@5401@@
The second is “proverb,” in Hebrew מָשָׁל (mashal). Verse 22 says: “what is this proverb that you have.” This word is also often translated “parable” or “proverb,” and it is a short sentence that is easy to remember. A short sentence can carry wisdom, and it can also become a wall that blocks God’s word. Turned into life: is there a saying you often have on your lips? “Not so soon.” “Let’s wait and see.” “We will talk about it later.” Have these short sentences also put God’s word away in a drawer for you?
@@5402@@
The third is “word,” in Hebrew דָּבָר (davar). Verse 25 says: “the word that I speak (אֲדַבֵּר) will be performed.” This word is special: it means both word and thing. In Hebrew, what he says and what he does are the same word. So what God speaks is itself already becoming a thing. Turned into life: his word is not news. It is something happening. If you hear it today, step onto it today. You do not have to be anxious on his behalf, and you do not have to schedule his days. You only need to ask: what does this word want me to do today?
@@5403@@
Join the three words together, and that is the journey of this chapter: the sign (מוֹפֵת) is him speaking with a human body, the proverb (מָשָׁל) is people blocking his word with a short sentence, and the word (דָּבָר) is a thing that is being done as soon as he has spoken. People wanted to put his word away with a proverb, but he says: my word is my deed. This is the greatest comfort of the whole chapter, and also the greatest reminder.
@@5404@@
God set the prophet as a sign. Do we also have to live as a sign to others today?
@@5405@@
We are not being told to imitate the special charges of digging through the wall and covering the face. Those were given by God to Ezekiel, and no one today may hurt himself or his family in order to be a sign. What we can learn is something very plain: when people cannot take in words, they watch how you live. You do not need to put on a show. Just let your daily life be consistent: if you said you would forgive, really let go once; if you said you would give thanks, really open your mouth once. A sermon like this does not need a wall dug through. It needs a person willing to be seen. If someone makes things hard for you because of your witness, please find a pastor and companions to support you.
@@5406@@
The first deviation is to treat 12:21–28 as a basis for making timetables and counting days. The text says that the word will be fulfilled. It does not give a schedule, and not one verse tells anyone to count. We want the congregation to trust his word more, not to be more eager to predict.
@@5407@@
The second deviation is to preach the “eating with quaking” of verses 18 and 19 as meaning that people who cannot eat or sleep are being judged. Verse 19 speaks of a land made desolate by violence. It is not judging an anxious person. An anxious person must first be protected, not blamed. We want people to dare to come before him more, not to blame themselves more.
@@5408@@
The third deviation is to say to someone who has long had no answer: your faith is not enough. What chapter 12 rebukes are those who treat “not yet” as “never,” not the weary. We should first admit that the weariness of waiting is real, and then together trust his word again.
@@5409@@
The fourth deviation is to preach the prophet’s picture of digging through the wall and covering his face as an action to imitate. This was a special charge God gave to the prophet. It is not telling the congregation to do the same, and above all no one should be allowed to hurt himself or his family. We want the congregation to learn his obedience, not to copy his movements.
@@5410@@
The fifth deviation is to preach the picture of the king caught in the snare and covering his face as a prediction about some person or nation today. This passage agrees with the historical record. It is history, not a prophecy about people now. These notes name no modern place and put no label on anyone. We want the congregation to be more humble, not better at judging.
@@5411@@
The first question: we are very used to saying “later,” “let’s see,” “when the children are grown,” “when I retire.” These words sound steady, but they often push God’s word to a very distant place. The people in verse 27 said that it was for many days to come. In Chinese families this kind of postponement can easily put on the coat of filial duty, caring for the family, and being safe. You can check yourself this way: is there one thing I know I should do, such as apologizing, visiting, giving, or talking with family about faith, that I have been saying “later” about for more than a year? Choose only one, take the first step this week, and write down the date.
@@5412@@
The second question: we are very used to reasoning with family. After ten years of talking, the other person still does not listen, so we change to sighing, the cold shoulder, and never bringing it up again. The God of 12:2 did not tell the prophet to speak louder, and did not tell him to be silent. He told him to change the way: live it out in front of them with his whole person. You can start this way: choose one family member you have never been able to persuade, stop saying that old sentence this month, and do one small thing instead, something for him that he did not ask for. Say his name and the date. When words cannot get in, love still can.
@@5413@@
I keep putting off what I know I should do. Is this postponement? How can I tell?
@@5414@@
You can tell this way: procrastination often comes with guilt, and postponement often comes with reasons. The procrastinator says: I know I should, I just have not done it yet. The postponer says: that is for later, and now is not the time. The people in verse 27 used the second kind. It sounds very reasonable, so it is harder to notice. You can ask yourself: if a person I trust heard my reason, would it seem reasonable to him? If even I need a long explanation, it is probably postponement. There is no need to blame yourself. Choose the smallest first step and do it today, and leave the rest to him.

VII. Living Out 321: Listen Today, Do It Today
@@5416@@
Jesus is my Role Model. The prophet spoke God’s word with his body. Jesus spoke who he is with his whole life. He walked out of his own home, took up the cross, and walked that road for us. When words cannot get in, the example is not to speak louder, but to live it out.
@@5417@@
The Bible is my Standard. I read chapter 12 not to settle the question of “when,” but to let this passage ask me: what have I heard today? Verse 28 says: none of my words will be deferred any more. The standard is not my calculation. It is the word he has spoken.
@@5418@@
The Holy Spirit is my Guide. The having eyes and not seeing, having ears and not hearing of verse 2 is exactly where the Holy Spirit heals. I ask the Holy Spirit to open my eyes and open my ears, and each day I ask only one question: today, which thing do I most want to postpone? He does not guide me by pressing, but by gentle reminders.
@@5419@@
Let Jesus be King. They placed his word in many days to come, in times far off. That is the power for me to decide when to listen. Selflessness is putting this power down. Letting Jesus be King is listening today, and taking the first step today.
@@5420@@
Let Jesus receive all the glory. If I obey today, it is not because I am quick to respond, but because his word is reliable. I do not say I have great faith. I only say: he spoke, and it is done. The glory goes to the Lord who speaks and it is done, and not a bit is kept for myself.
@@5421@@
To build God’s system. Verse 25 says that what he speaks will be performed. This is the root of a reliable system. God’s system is not built on my savings, my position, or my grasp, but on the faithfulness of him who speaks and it is done. First life, then relationships, then ministry: first listen today, and only then will there be a group of people who remind and accompany one another.
@@5422@@
What does letting Jesus be King have to do with taking the first step today?
@@5423@@
The relation is very direct. Postponing is really saying: when to listen and when to do is up to me. That seat belongs to the King. Letting Jesus be King does not mean you must obey completely overnight. It means giving the power to decide the time back to him, and taking one step today. This step can be very small: a message of apology, a visit, an offering, one time opening your mouth. You do not need to understand everything first. You only need to take the first step. Be at ease: he will not ask you to walk it all at once, and he will not leave you on the road. He is the King, and on the road you walk he will light up the next step.
@@5424@@
The proud type says: when to listen and when to do, I will decide myself. The text says to him: that is for many days to come, and has nothing to do with me. This is what people find easiest to say. A proud person leaves his position, and even wants to set his own times for God’s time. To return to one’s position is to admit: the one who speaks is he, and I am only the one who listens. You can first say one sentence to God: Lord, today I will take the first step.
@@5425@@
The fearful type says: if I really do as his word says, the price is too great, and I am afraid I cannot bear it. The prophet in chapter 12 paid a price, yet God also left a “may be,” and also left a few men. You do not need to see everything at once. Just do the step in front of you, and he will be on the road, lighting up the next step.
@@5426@@
The shame type says: I have postponed for so long, God must be disappointed in me, so let it go. Verse 28 says: none of my words will be deferred any more. It is people who delay, not his word. His word has been waiting for you all along. You can begin with the smallest thing, and you do not need to atone first for your past postponing.
@@5427@@
The wounded type says: I have waited so long and my prayers have had no answer, and I dare not hope again. God has not rebuked your weariness. What he says is: my word is reliable. You do not need to force yourself to have faith. You can first say honestly: Lord, I am tired. Then let a brother or sister wait with you. The one who accompanies should not be in a hurry to say “you must have faith.” He only needs to say: I am here.

The simplest thing is to take a sheet of paper today and write one sentence: the “later” I have said the longest is ______. No need to polish it, and no need to show it to anyone. Then say to God: Lord, today I listen. Next, choose one small action you can do today: send a short message to someone you have long wanted to contact, saying: I thought of you, and may you have peace. Or finish half of a small thing you have put off for a long time, right now. This action is the answer of chapter 12: not first understanding everything, but first taking today’s step. At the next small group meeting, just say: this week I did one thing. There is no need to say what it was.
@@5438@@
Chapter 12 begins with a pile of baggage and ends with one sentence. At the beginning, the prophet piled baggage at the door by day and dug through his own wall by night. In the middle, the king went out in the dark, and eating and drinking were done with trembling. At the end, a proverb was put to an end, and this sentence took its place: none of my words will be deferred any more. From baggage to word: he went from speaking with a body to accomplishing with words.
@@5439@@
In this chapter, God wants us to see that he does not give up speaking to those who will not listen. God wants us to put down postponement and listen today, and he wants us to remember that his word is more reliable than our weariness. That God, in Jesus, walked out of his home, took up the cross, and with his whole life spoke who he is.
@@5440@@
So please remember the three key points of this lesson: baggage, sign, near. The focus of this lesson is not on how much you know or how much you have calculated, not on how long you have waited, and not on whether others listen, but on Jesus, who speaks and it is done, and who also sees our weariness.

<p><strong>What does the storm refer to?</strong></p><ul><li><strong>Literal weather:</strong> there really is a stormy wind, a downpour, and hail.</li><li><strong>A figure for invasion:</strong> a storm is often used to picture an enemy army coming, like the storm from the north in 1:4.</li><li><strong>The whole picture of judgment:</strong> God’s judgment has heaven and earth and all things as his instruments.</li></ul><blockquote><p><strong>The best understanding:</strong> The text does not ask us to choose. It asks us to look at the subject: “I” will make the storm split it open. The storm is sent by him, and its target is “the wall” and “those who plastered it,” not all the walls in the world, and not all storms.</p></blockquote>
@@5517@@
<p><strong>When the storm comes, white paint will not speak for the wall.</strong></p><p>Verse 14 is very concrete: it is brought down to the ground, and the foundation is uncovered. What the plaster covered was the foundation. Only when the wall falls does anyone see, for the first time, what it was standing on.</p><p>The final picture is the declaration of verse 15: the wall and those who plastered it are no more. Those who plastered did not stand outside the wall. They were part of the wall, and their end is the same as the wall’s. Verse 16 translates the picture into reality: those who plastered are the prophets of Israel, and the wall is the vision of peace they saw for Jerusalem.</p><p>Notice that “wrath” appears three times in one verse: חֵמָה, אַף, חֵמָה. This is not a God who forgot love. This is a God who still protects his own people when a man-made wall is given a stamp of approval. His anger is aimed at those who plaster, not at those who want a wall.</p><p>Not every storm is judgment. The storm here points to one particular wall.</p><p>Landing: sometimes a storm in life washes the white paint away, so that for the first time you see what you have been standing on. After the plaster is stripped off, what is left of your foundation?</p>

<p><strong>What are the “pillows” and the “kerchiefs”?</strong></p><ul><li><strong>Magic objects:</strong> objects tied on the wrist or draped over the head to cast spells, used to divine or to manipulate people.</li><li><strong>Figure:</strong> a false comfort that gives people ease, like a pillow.</li></ul><p>כְּסָתוֹת and מִסְפָּחוֹת both appear only in this chapter, and their meaning cannot be determined. The text itself tells us three things.</p><blockquote><p><strong>The best understanding:</strong> First, these things touch the body, the wrists and the head; second, the target is “lives”; third, God’s accusation is not about the objects, but about “hunting.” The meaning of the words is uncertain, but the charge is clear.</p></blockquote>
@@5520@@
<p><strong>What they sewed was cloth. What they hunted was people.</strong></p><p>The audience of this passage is the women prophets, and the original says they too prophesy “out of their own heart” (the same as the male prophets in verse 2). The charge is not about gender. The charge is about source and use: coming out of their own heart, yet used to hunt the lives of others.</p><p>In the picture there are wrists and heads, pillows and kerchiefs, and persons of every stature, with no one an exception. In the end, it is a kind of control: who will die and who may live is for them to say.</p><p>Verse 19 reveals the price: for handfuls of barley and pieces of bread. The original is שַׁעֲלֵי שְׂעֹרִים, “handfuls of barley.” For this small price they “kill the souls who should not die, and save the souls alive who should not live.” Deciding who lives and who dies is his place.</p><p>The last sentence does not let the listeners go either: “by your lying to my people who listen to lies.” There are those who sell, and there are those who buy.</p><p>Landing: anyone who lives off other people’s fear or hope, including those on the platform, should put this passage on his own table. Ask yourself: are my words setting people free, or holding on to them?</p>

<p><strong>How should “לְפֹרְחוֹת” be read?</strong></p><ul><li><strong>“Like birds that fly”:</strong> the hunted lives are caught as birds are caught.</li><li><strong>“To make them fly away”:</strong> making people’s lives scattered and unsettled.</li></ul><p>The Piel verb שִׁלַּח is used in Lev 14:7 for “letting go” a captured bird. God’s action is exactly the opposite: they catch birds, and he lets the birds go.</p><blockquote><p><strong>The best understanding:</strong> Both readings draw the same picture. The point is the second half: the hunted birds will be let go.</p></blockquote>
@@5523@@
<p><strong>God himself cuts those knots.</strong></p><p>His response is one verb after another: tear, let go, tear, deliver. All of it is what “I” will do. Those who are bound do not have to untie themselves first.</p><p>He will “tear.” Tear is the word for tearing clothes, the action of grief. He pulls the pillows off their arms and tears the kerchiefs from their heads, not out of ease, but to rescue those who were hunted.</p><p>More beautiful is שָׁלַח, “let go.” In Lev 14:7 this verb is used to let a living bird go free. They used charm bands to catch people, as one catches birds; he will let these birds go, one by one.</p><p>Look once more: “my people.” He says that these hunted ones are his. The hunters thought what they held in their hands was someone else’s life, but he says: that is mine.</p><p>Landing: if you have ever been bound by fear by a “spiritual person,” or trapped by words like “if you don’t do this, disaster will come,” this passage is said to you: he himself comes to cut. You do not have to prove first that you have enough faith before being set free.</p>
