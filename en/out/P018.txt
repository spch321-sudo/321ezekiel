I. The Elders Sitting Before Me (14:1–5)
@@6176@@
Brothers and sisters, peace to you. A friend came to me one day and asked very earnestly, “Pastor, I want to ask God whether I should buy that house.” I said, “Good, let’s pray together.” When we finished praying, I casually asked, “Have you signed the contract?” He lowered his head and said, “I’ve already paid the deposit.” I did not laugh at him, because I have done the same thing. We often come to inquire with the answer already in hand, hoping only that God will stamp it afterwards. Today we read Ezekiel 14, and it begins with just such a scene: some of the elders of Israel came to me and sat before me.
@@6177@@
Please imagine the picture. They were elders, with status and age, sitting properly before the prophet, and they looked very respectful and very spiritual. But in verses 2 and 3, Yahweh’s word came to the prophet, saying: “Son of man, these men have taken their idols into their heart, and put the stumbling block of their iniquity before their face. Should I be inquired of at all by them?” Before they even opened their mouths, God had already seen what was in their hearts. The most remarkable thing about this passage is that the idols are not in the temple and not on the mountain, but in the heart.
@@6178@@
The Hebrew action behind the words “taken into the heart” is very concrete: it means “to lift up.” Idols do not run in by themselves; we lift them up bit by bit. At first it is a good thing: a job, a relationship, a sense of security, a very natural hope. Later it is lifted higher and higher, until it is so high that “if I lose it, I cannot live,” and that place is God’s place. When the “stumbling block” is “put before the face,” it means that every time he looks up, what he sees is that, not God.
@@6179@@
Verses 4 and 5 are very heavy words. God says: everyone who takes his idols into his heart and comes to the prophet, I will answer him according to the multitude of his idols, that I may take the house of Israel in their own heart, because they are all estranged from me through their idols. It means that to this kind of inquiry God does not give a pretty answer, but responds to him with the very thing in his heart, until he sees: I have become estranged from God. Please note the last words, “estranged from me.” God’s sorrow is not that rules were broken, but that the relationship was cut.
@@6180@@
There is something in my heart that I really want. Can I still pray and ask God? Or must I drive it away first?
@@6181@@
You can pray, and you begin with exactly this. What Ezekiel 14 rebukes is not “having something you want in your heart,” but “taking it into the heart as a god, then pretending it is not there, and still asking God to stamp it for me.” Honesty is the first step of turning back. You can say: Lord, I really want that thing, I am afraid of losing it, and right now I do not know whether it is a gift or my throne. Please tell me. Then do one thing: say your “deposit” out loud, and do not pretend you have not decided yet. God is not frightened by our desires. What he cares about is that we stop telling him the truth, because what he wants is a relationship, not a stamp.

II. Turning Back and His Own Answer (14:6–11)
@@6183@@
But God did not close the door. In verse 6, his voice suddenly changes: “Therefore tell the house of Israel, ‘The Lord Yahweh says: “Return, and turn yourselves from your idols! Turn away your faces from all your abominations.”’” Look, the word of judgment was not yet finished, and the call came. God’s first move is not an interrogation but a call: return. This is the turning point of the whole chapter.
@@6184@@
Verse 7 goes on: everyone who separates himself from me and takes his idols into his heart, whether of the house of Israel or of the strangers who live in Israel, and comes to the prophet to inquire of me for himself, I Yahweh will answer him by myself. The words “by myself” are very heavy. It means: not through the prophet, not through anyone, God comes himself. But verse 8 also says that if a person insists on not turning back, God will set his face against that man and make him a sign. Look, these two things stand side by side: for the one who turns back, God answers himself; for the one who does not turn back, God sets his face against him. His heart is to keep the door open.
@@6185@@
Verse 9 is the hardest verse in this chapter: if the prophet is deceived and speaks a word, I Yahweh let that prophet be deceived. We must say honestly that this verse has a difficulty, and interpreters understand it differently. Some say that God gives over those who insist on not listening to the lies they themselves have chosen (compare the “gave them up” of Romans 1:24). Others say that this is a common Old Testament way of speaking, attributing everything to God’s sovereignty, and does not mean that God is the source of deception. Whichever it is, the text itself has two clear boundaries. First, “They will bear their iniquity” (verse 10), so the responsibility is on people. Second, verse 11 explains the purpose: that the house of Israel may no more go astray from me, but that they may be my people, and I may be their God. God does not want to destroy people. He wants to bring them back.
@@6186@@
Verse 11 is the gentlest sentence of the whole passage: that they may be my people, and I may be their God. This is covenant language that appears again and again throughout the whole book of Ezekiel. So the end point of turning back is not a checklist of cleared idols, but a relationship: you are my people, and I am your God. We do not first clean ourselves up and then become qualified to be answered by God. God first said “Return,” and only then do we have the strength to turn around.
@@6187@@
Ezekiel 14:9 says God “let that prophet be deceived.” Does that make God the source of evil?
@@6188@@
No. This is one of the hardest passages in the Old Testament, and we should not pretend it is simple. A few points can help us. First, God is never the author of sin. James 1:13 says that God tempts no one. Second, the background of this sentence is that people kept refusing God’s call (verse 6), and in the end God handed them over to the road they chose. This is the manner of judgment, not its beginning. Third, verse 10 stresses that they will bear their iniquity, so the responsibility is on people. Fourth, verse 11 explains God’s purpose: that people no longer go astray, but come back to him. If you have questions as you read this verse, you can honestly say to God: Lord, I do not understand it all, but I believe that you are good. This is faith, not weakness.

III. The Three Men and the Remnant (14:12–23)
@@6190@@
In verse 12, Yahweh’s word came to the prophet again. This time God spoke on a larger scale: when a land sins against me by committing a trespass, I stretch out my hand on it, and break the staff of its bread, that is, cut off its supply of food. Then God put forward three men: Noah, Daniel, and Job. All three are widely recognized as righteous in the Bible. Noah was kept safe in the flood, Daniel refused to defile himself in the king’s palace, and Job was called blameless and upright by God in the midst of disaster. About this Daniel, some scholars suggest he may be a righteous man from ancient Near Eastern tradition, and others say he is the prophet who lived at the same time as Ezekiel. These notes do not make either view a condition of faith. The point is: these three are righteous men whom everyone esteemed.
@@6191@@
But God’s word is very clear: though these three men were in it, they would deliver only their own souls by their righteousness. God said it four times in a row, with the four judgments of famine, evil animals, sword, and pestilence, and each time it was the same sentence: though these three men were in it, as I live, they would deliver neither sons nor daughters, but only themselves would be delivered. This is a very hard sentence. What it shatters is a hope in the people’s hearts at that time: there are good people in Jerusalem, there is the blessing the ancestors left, there are some spiritual people holding things up for us, so I do not have to turn back myself.
@@6192@@
Please note that these verses are not saying that intercession is useless, nor that other people’s prayers do nothing for you. What they say is: righteousness cannot be transferred. You can transfer a sum of money to someone else, but you cannot transfer your trust to someone else. Parents’ faith, a pastor’s spirituality, the church’s reputation are all grace and help God gives us, but when we come to God, every person must stand there in person. This sentence is very heavy, and also very fair, because it is at the same time a freedom: no one’s failure can block my way back to God myself.
@@6193@@
We must handle the sentence “neither sons nor daughters” with great care. It absolutely does not say that the loss of a child is the parents’ fault, nor that a child’s faith is entirely the responsibility of the parents’ spirituality. It speaks of a principle of judgment: no one can escape by another person’s righteousness. It is not an evaluation of any parent or child. If you are grieving over a child or a close relative, this passage has not come to condemn you. You can tell God your pain, and he does not ask you to carry one more layer of guilt.
@@6194@@
Finally, in verses 21 to 23, God said: how much more when I send my four severe judgments on Jerusalem! Yet there will be left a remnant in it that will be carried out, both sons and daughters. They will come out to you, and you will see their way and their doings, and you will be comforted concerning all the evil that I have brought on Jerusalem. The tone of these verses suddenly changes. At the very end of the judgment, God leaves a “yet.” These survivors are not righteous people. They too were brought out, and they too carry wounds. Their existence lets those who were already exiled see that what God did was not without cause. This is not a standard answer that can be said to every sufferer, but it was a comfort God spoke to these elders: God has not forgotten, and even in judgment he still leaves people.
@@6195@@
The passage says a righteous person can only save himself. Does that mean my prayers for my family are useless?
@@6196@@
No. What this passage says is that no one can use another’s righteousness in place of returning to God personally. It does not say that intercession has no meaning. Elsewhere the Bible says that when Moses interceded for the people, God turned away from the evil he had said he would do (Exodus 32:11–14), and that Jesus also intercedes for us (Hebrews 7:25). So you can keep praying for your family, and at the same time understand this: your prayer puts them before God, but it does not turn back in their place; turning back is something they must do themselves, and something God will answer himself. In this way you do not have to carry the burden of “I did not pray well enough, so he was not saved.” Give your prayers to God, and give him to God.
@@6197@@
If I have lost a child or a loved one, and I read “neither sons nor daughters,” what should I do?
@@6198@@
Please stop first. You are not the person this verse is meant to condemn. This verse is God’s declaration about the judgment on Jerusalem at that time, and it says that no one can escape judgment by another’s righteousness. It is not an evaluation of any family’s loss, and it certainly does not say you are at fault. You may skip this verse, or you may set it aside and read it when you have the strength. God is with you, and he does not want you to carry one more layer of guilt. Please find a pastor or friend you trust to accompany you while you speak this pain out. If grief has already affected your sleep, eating, and daily life, please also seek help from a professional. This is not weakness. It is loving yourself, and it is loving the Lord.
@@6199@@
IV. Light from the Original: Heart, Turn Back, Remnant (14:3, 6, 22)
@@6200@@
The first word is “heart,” Hebrew לֵב (lev), which appears again and again in verses 3, 4, and 5. When the Hebrews say “heart,” they do not mean only feelings. It is the center of the whole person: thoughts, will, desires, and decisions are all here. So “taken into the heart” means not only liking it, but letting it sit at the center of my decisions. The idol of Ezekiel 14 is not in the act of worship, but in this position. Thirty seconds into life: when you make a decision today, whom do you ask first? That place of “asking first” is the throne of the heart.
@@6201@@
The second word is “turn back,” Hebrew שׁוּבוּ (shuvu), verse 6. This is an imperative, from the root שׁוּב, which means to turn around, to come back. In the Bible this is the most commonly used word for repentance. Please note that it is not “correcting behavior,” but “turning around”: the body is still in the same place, but the face has turned in another direction. Thirty seconds into life: you do not have to wait until you have cleared out all the idols to turn around. Today, right now, turn your face, and say to him: Lord, I am here.
@@6202@@
The third word is “remnant,” Hebrew פְּלֵטָה (pelețah), verse 22. This word means those who escape, those who are preserved. In the judgments of Ezekiel it is not a cold statistic but a seed laid in advance: in the blackest place, God still leaves a road for his people. Thirty seconds into life: if you feel that you are one of the “left behind,” covered with wounds, left in the ruins after the disaster, this word tells you that you were not overlooked. You were preserved.
@@6203@@
Put the three Hebrew words together: what do they mean for me today?
@@6204@@
The three words join into one road. “Heart” (לֵב) is where the problem is: the throne has been taken. “Turn back” (שׁוּבוּ) is the invitation: you can turn around right now. “Remnant” (פְּלֵטָה) is the hope: even after walking through judgment, God still leaves people for his people. The starting point of this road is not that we become better, but that God first says “Return.” You can practice this way: this week, each day, be quiet for one minute and do only three things. First, say out loud what it is in your heart that you cannot let go of. Second, turn around, and say to God: I am here. Third, believe what he said: I will answer him myself. The original is not new knowledge, but a small path we can walk.
@@6205@@
The first deviation is to preach “idols in the heart” as an accusation against every troubled person. Ezekiel 14:3 is spoken to a group of elders who came to inquire while carrying idols, not to every person crying out in pain. We want the hurting person to receive one more invitation, “God is waiting for me,” and not one more burden, “Is there an idol somewhere in me?”
@@6206@@
The second deviation is to use verses 14 to 20 to say “other people’s prayers do nothing for you.” This passage says that righteousness cannot be transferred. It is not denying intercession. We want the congregation to be more willing to pray for others, and at the same time more willing to stand before God themselves, and not to become more afraid to intercede for others.
@@6207@@
The third deviation is to quote “neither sons nor daughters” to someone who has lost a child or a family member. This sentence must never be used to imply that anyone is at fault for a loss. The preacher must say plainly: this does not say that a child’s loss is the parents’ responsibility. If someone in the room has lost a loved one, they should not leave with one more layer of guilt.
@@6208@@
The fourth deviation is to take the four judgments of sword, famine, evil animals, and pestilence and match them to any current event. The text is a judgment on Jerusalem at that time. It does not give a verdict on present events, and it is not for working out a timetable. We want the congregation to love the Lord more and be more patient, and not to be more afraid and more eager to match things up.
@@6209@@
The fifth deviation is to preach 14:9, “let that prophet be deceived,” as if God were the author of sin, or to avoid the verse altogether. The difficulty of this verse must be said honestly, while holding to the whole flow of the text: the responsibility is on people (verse 10), and the purpose is that the people no longer go astray, but come back to him (verse 11). Idols also should not be preached only as “worshiping wood and stone.” Whatever takes God’s place, including good things, may become an idol.
@@6210@@
How do I know whether something in my heart is a “gift” or an “idol”?
@@6211@@
You can use three questions to tell. First, if I lose it, am I sad, or do I feel the sky is falling? Sadness is normal; the sky falling means the throne has been taken. Second, to get it, would I be willing to lie, go against my conscience, or hurt others? Third, can I, in prayer, put it before God and say, “You may take it,” without feeling that my whole self collapses? If the answers are no, there is no need to blame yourself. It is only seeing. Once you have seen, you can turn around. You can first ask yourself: is this thing a gift I thank God for, or a god I am asking God to protect for me? The difference is here: a gift brings me closer to God, and an idol makes me more afraid of losing.
@@6212@@
First question: we are very used to depending on the righteousness of the “family.” We say: we are a Christian family; my mother prays very well; my grandmother served in the church her whole life. These are all true graces, and worth giving thanks for. But Ezekiel 14:14 reminds us: righteousness cannot be transferred. You can receive much help in the prayers of your elders, but in the end you still have to say one sentence yourself: Lord, I am yours. You can begin this way: this week, write down one prayer that belongs only to you, without borrowing anyone’s words, say it to God, and let a friend know.
@@6213@@
Second question: we are very used to lifting “good things” onto the throne. For the children, for the family, for face, to set the parents’ minds at ease, we can sacrifice everything, and this looks great, and very loving. But when “for the children” becomes “if my child is not well, I cannot go on living,” the child is no longer a gift, but a god on the throne. You can examine yourself this way: for which good thing have I recently neglected God? You do not need to give it up. You only need to invite it down from the throne, and put it back in the place of a gift.
@@6214@@
Giving everything for my children and family: could that also be an idol? How should I adjust?
@@6215@@
It could be, but the key is not how much you give; it is the position. Loving your children and giving yourself for your family is God’s command. The problem appears when I treat my child’s success and my child’s safety as my own security and worth, and when something goes wrong with him, I lose my balance. You can adjust this way: First, when you pray for your child each day, add one sentence: Lord, you love him more deeply than I do, and I give him to you. Second, set aside a time just for your own relationship with God, not for anyone else. Third, if your child is suffering, do not blame yourself, and do not suppress it; find someone you trust and tell them your worry. This is not loving your child less. It is putting your child back into God’s hand.

VII. Living Out 321: Give the Throne Back
@@6217@@
Jesus is my Role Model. When he was tempted in the wilderness, the devil showed him all the kingdoms of the world and their glory, and he answered: “You shall worship the Lord your God, and you shall serve him only.” In his heart there was only one place, and he gave it only to the Father. When we follow him, we do not frantically tear down every idol; we learn his way and keep the throne for the Father alone.
@@6218@@
The Bible is my Standard. The “Return” of Ezekiel 14:6 is God’s word, not a human suggestion. The thoughts in my heart, including “I have already decided, I only want God to stamp it,” must all be measured under the Scripture. One verse of Scripture outweighs a thousand of my reasons.
@@6219@@
The Holy Spirit is my Guide. Idols are often hidden in the most natural places, and I cannot see them myself. It is the Holy Spirit who lets me see the place in my heart, and it is also the Holy Spirit who gives me the strength to turn around. Each day, be quiet for one minute and ask: Lord, what is it in my heart right now that I cannot let go of?
@@6220@@
Let Jesus be King. Selflessness is not working hard to tear down idols, but letting Jesus sit on the throne, so that everything else returns to its place and becomes a gift. My work, my relationships, my hopes are all good things God gave. When he is King, these good things no longer need to be gods.
@@6221@@
Let Jesus receive all the glory. If an idol has been invited down, do not say “I finally quit it,” but say: the Lord answered me himself. It is not the victory of my willpower, but his call and his grace. All the glory goes to the Lord who answered himself.
@@6222@@
To build God’s system. Idols in the heart also become dark holes in a system. In God’s system, life comes first, then relationships, then ministry: first deal with the throne in the heart, and only then talk about what to do for God; and every person stands before God himself, not relying on another’s righteousness. A community like this is a small God’s system on earth.
@@6223@@
The proud type says: “I know what is in my heart best, and I do not need anyone to remind me.” The text says to him: Should I be inquired of at all by them? Pride is leaving one’s own position, lifting what one wants onto the throne, and then asking God to stamp it. Returning to one’s position is saying one true sentence: Lord, my deposit has already been paid. You do not need to put it all down at once. First admit that it is there.
@@6224@@
The fearful type says: “If I hand it over, I will lose everything.” God says: Return, and I will answer you myself. The fearful type needs to learn to replace “I fear losing it” with “I have a God who answers me himself.” You can begin with the smallest thing: take one thing you cannot let go of, and in prayer, gently set it in God’s hand for one minute.
@@6225@@
The ashamed type says: “There is too much in my heart that should not be there, and God would not want to answer me.” Ezekiel 14:7 says that whether Israelite or stranger who lives in Israel, everyone who turns back to inquire, I will answer myself. The one who turns back does not have to clean himself up first. The ashamed type does not have to hide until he is tidy. Just today, come as you are, and turn around.
@@6226@@
The wounded type says: “I have lost a child or the person who mattered most, and I do not want to hear anything about idols or righteous men.” God has not blamed you for your wound, and he does not ask you to carry one more layer of guilt. The wounded can slowly find someone trustworthy and tell their story. Those who accompany should not rush to say “there is a reason for everything,” and should not say “you must have faith.” Just say: “I am here, and I am with you.”
@@6227@@
First, each day spend two minutes before the Lord and say honestly: “Right now, what I cannot let go of in my heart is ____.” Do not rush to drive it away; only set it before him; write down the date.
@@6228@@
Second, find one thing you have “already decided and only want God to stamp.” This week bring it back before God and say: Lord, I am willing to hear you. And ask a brother or sister to accompany you; tell them the name and the time.
