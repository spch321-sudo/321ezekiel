The most special thing about chapter 32 is that it takes words of judgment and writes them as a lament. At the beginning, God tells the prophet to take up a lament over Pharaoh king of Egypt, calling him a great fish that muddies the rivers. In the middle, God says the waters will finally become clear and people will know that he is Yahweh. At the end is a very large and very quiet burial ground, where the nations lie one after another, and those who once terrified the living no longer move. From song to pit, from terror to stillness, this is a journey God takes without delight, yet he cannot help but speak of it.

<p><strong>When the trumpet sounds, the responsibility passes to the one who hears.</strong></p><p>This passage divides the responsibility: the watchman is to blow the trumpet, and the people are to respond.
