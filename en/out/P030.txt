I. A Family Photo on the Wall (23:1–10)
@@10212@@
Brothers and sisters, peace to you. I once visited an old sister’s home. On the living room wall hung a yellowed photograph of two little girls standing side by side, in the same floral dresses, with the same bangs, even missing teeth in the same places. The old sister pointed at the photo and said, “These are my two daughters. One lives close by. The other hasn’t come home in many years.” Her eyes did not leave the photo as she spoke. I did not ask more. But I thought: that photo has hung there for so many years, and that is a mother’s heart. The name is still on the wall, and the seat is still at the table. Today we read Ezekiel chapter 23, and it is a family photo like this, except that what hangs on the wall is God’s heart.
@@10213@@
Let me make one thing clear first. Verses 2 to 4 say there were two women, daughters of one mother, the elder named Oholah and the younger named Oholibah. Verse 4 itself reveals the answer to the riddle: Oholah is Samaria, and Oholibah is Jerusalem. So this is a parable. God uses it to speak of two cities, not of any one person. The wording of this parable is very blunt. We will not enlarge the picture, we will not explain it word by word, and we will not use it to judge anyone’s past, anyone’s marriage, or anyone’s gender. If this chapter touches somewhere very deep in you, you may pause. You do not have to finish reading, you do not have to say anything aloud, and you do not have to forgive anything first.
