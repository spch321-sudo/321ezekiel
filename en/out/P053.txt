A good gate needs both.</p><p>Landing: Does your home, your church, your heart have a “gate facing east”? Perhaps it is a direction you once closed and, because you were hurt, no longer open. <p><strong><strong>This chapter ends standing at the door of the house.</strong></strong></p><p>The whole chapter ends at one place: the porch in front of the house. Here the prophet measured the posts, the length and width of the porch, saw the steps, and also saw one pillar on each side.</p><p>This is an ending that “stands at the door.” He did not go in, and the text does not take us in. The outer court, the inner court, the gates, the tables and the rooms before were all for this moment: standing before the house.</p><p>Notice also: “by the steps by which they went up to it.” Steps again. From the seven steps of the outer gate, to the eight of the inner gate, to the steps of the porch, the design of the whole house goes upward all the way.</p><p>“One on this side, and another on that side”: two pillars stand at the door, like two silent guards, and also like an open arch. When you go in, you pass between them.</p><p>The end of the chapter has no conclusion and no lesson. This is a chapter that leaves white space: the measuring is done, we stand before the door, and in the next chapter, inside that door, there will be more.</p><p>Landing: If today you also feel you are only standing at the door and have not yet gone in, that too is a place of being prepared. After reading this chapter, do not hurry to grab an answer. Just remember: the prophet was told “see with your eyes, hear with your ears, and set your heart on it,” and we stand here following him.</p>

I. Brought to the Mountain: See with Your Eyes, Hear with Your Ears (40:1–4)
@@18302@@
Brothers and sisters, peace to you. On the doorframe of your old home, was there ever a mark for height? I know an elder who, every Lunar New Year when his children came home, would call them to the doorframe, have them stand straight against it, draw a line lightly with a pencil, and write beside it the name and the year. Later the children went off one by one to far places, and the old house was sold. He said that what he could least bear to part with was not the house, but that doorframe, because on it were the marks, one by one, measured, proving that this home had really existed. Today we read chapter 40, a chapter that begins with “measuring,” and the one who opens it is a man who has counted days for twenty-five years in a foreign land.
@@18303@@
The date in verse 1 is very specific: the twenty-fifth year of our captivity, the fourteenth year after the city of Jerusalem was struck. The prophet did not say, “I saw.” First he said, “We have counted how long.” You are in a foreign land, counting the same day year after year; after counting for so long, you no longer ask when you will go back, you have only grown used to not going back. This is the deepest loss: not pain, but habit. And in this very year when going home was least possible, verse 1 says: Yahweh’s hand was on me, and he brought me there. Please notice that verb, and that its subject is God. The prophet had no boat, no road, no permit; he was brought there. Sometimes grace is not that he builds the road; it is that he himself brings you across.
@@18304@@
Verse 2 says that in the visions of God he was brought into the land of Israel and set down on a very high mountain; on the south side of the mountain was something like the frame of a city. The word “set down” has the meaning of putting down, of causing to rest; he did not climb up, he was set there. Then he said a very honest word: “like.” He does not say “a city”; he says “something like the frame of a city.” He knew that what the eyes saw was bigger than what the mouth could say. In the nine chapters that follow there are many measurements, many gates, many courts, and the readings of the different views are not all the same. Some say it is a house that will be built; some say it uses words the exiles could understand to describe the completeness of God’s presence; some say it is fulfilled in Christ and the church. We set them side by side, respect one another, do not add to the vision details the prophet did not state, and certainly do not arrange any timetable. Because the main figure of these nine chapters is not the measurements; it is God’s presence.
