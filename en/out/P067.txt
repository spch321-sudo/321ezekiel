@@23963@@
The warning shows what is real and what is false
@@23964@@
Some think the warning is to show what true faith is. True believers are kept, and the warning is a tool of God’s keeping.
@@23965@@
The Old Testament setting
@@23966@@
The text speaks to the Old Testament people and explains the relation between obedience to the covenant and life and death. It cannot be applied directly as the whole of New Testament teaching on salvation.

No need to calculate times

To the listeners at that time

Who are “the shepherds of Israel”? May this chapter be used to judge church leaders today?
@@24008@@
1. Do the “shepherds” of chapter 34 mean the kings and officials of that time, or do they also include religious leaders? How can church leaders and those who lead today read this chapter without making it a weapon of attack?
@@24009@@
Kings and officials
@@24010@@
Traditionally many scholars think “shepherd” in the ancient Near East often meant kings and rulers, and here it first refers to Judah’s kings and their officials.
@@24011@@
Also including religious leaders
@@24012@@
Some think the shepherds also mean priests, prophets, and others who led the people, so it can be extended to those who carry the responsibility of leading today.
@@24013@@
Applying the principle
@@24014@@
The text first speaks to the people of that time. The “principles of shepherding” in it can be used by any leader today to examine themselves, but not to attack a particular person.
@@24015@@
Our position in these notes: the conclusion of this chapter is not to find the bad shepherds, but that God himself will be the shepherd. First look at ourselves, and return to humility and to our place. We do not use it to attack anyone. In line with the issue “Dating and the Prophet: What Is “the Thirtieth Year,” and What Kind of Prophet Is This?”: the point is that the word of Yahweh came, not tracking down people.
