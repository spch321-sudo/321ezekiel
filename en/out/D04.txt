<p><strong>Even the wooden boards that cover the walls of this house are made to measure.</strong></p><p>Verses 16 and 17 are long and hard to read aloud. They list the thresholds, the windows, and the galleries, and then say that all around, from the ground up to the windows, everything was covered with wood, inside and out.</p><p>This one sentence is the outer garment of the whole house. The windows are “closed.” The Hebrew word carries the sense of “shut tight.” These windows are not for looking at the scenery outside. They let the light in, but they do not let what is outside come in.</p><p>Then comes the last phrase: “made to measure.” Even the wooden boards have their measurements. There is no careless place in this house.</p><p>This passage sounds as if it is speaking to the exiles: the temple you once saw was burned and torn down. This new one is different. Even a single board against the wall has been accounted for. You are not asked to memorize the numbers. You are asked to know that he does not work carelessly.</p><p>When you read verse 16, try following that line slowly. Start at the threshold, go up to the window frames, and then up to “the galleries around on their three stories, opposite the threshold.” The wood runs all the way up past the door. It is not one single point but a whole circle, a whole layer of covering, in the inner temple and the outer temple, inside and out, all around. The prophet stands inside and looks up. Everywhere he looks there are neat boards, and not one place is left bare and done in a careless way.</p><p><strong>Landing:</strong> Is there something you have felt was “not worth doing well,” so you did it carelessly? In God’s eyes, even the boards have their measurements. Do the smallest thing of your day well.</p>

Jesus is my Role Model. He said, “He calls his own sheep by name, and leads them out.” (John 10:3) He is not shouting from far away at a whole flock. He calls them one by one, by name. In chapter 48 the gates have names and the land has portions, and he is the same: he calls us one by one. He is the shepherd who both searches for us in person and remembers us in person. Those who follow him also learn to remember other people’s names.
