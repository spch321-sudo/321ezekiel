3. 44:7--9 forbids foreigners uncircumcised in heart and flesh to enter the sanctuary. Is this rejecting people of other nations? How does it exist together with Isaiah 56:3--8 and Ephesians 2:19?
@@24498@@
The ones rebuked are the Israelites who brought them in
@@24499@@
The point is that the Israelites did not keep charge of the holy things and passed the duty to others, and it is not aimed at the foreigners.
@@24500@@
“Heart and flesh” is the key
@@24501@@
The standard is that both heart and body turn to him, not one’s origin; foreigners who turn to God are accepted elsewhere in Scripture (Isa 56:6--7).
@@24502@@
A rule for the holy place in the Old Testament time
@@24503@@
This was the boundary of the holy place at that time; in the New Testament, Christ has torn down the wall (Eph 2:14).
@@24504@@
The position of these notes: the weight of this verse is that those who brought them in did not keep watch, not rejecting other peoples. In Christ there are no outsiders (Eph 2:19).
@@24505@@
Do not use this verse to make any newcomer, or anyone from a different background, feel “not good enough.” The door of the church is open to everyone who turns to him.

Do the marriage rules for priests (44:22) belittle widows and divorced women?
@@24507@@
4. Priests may not marry widows or divorced women. Is this a judgment on them? How do we read it today?
@@24508@@
A rule of separation
@@24509@@
It is only a limit on the priestly office, not a judgment on the women themselves.
@@24510@@
Symbolic cleanness
@@24511@@
It symbolizes that priests must keep single-hearted devotion and being set apart, and it does not say that women are unclean.
@@24512@@
A rule of the times
@@24513@@
It is a social rule of a particular time, and in the New Testament it has been surpassed by Christ’s grace.
@@24514@@
The position of these notes: this is a rule of separation for the priests, not a judgment on widows and divorced women. They are equally cared for before God (Exod 22:22; Ps 68:5).
@@24515@@
If you are a widow, or have been abandoned, this rule is not judging you. Please do not feel demoted because of it. You do not need to explain your story; he cares about you.

“They shall teach my people the difference between the holy and the common”: how does the church do this today?
@@24517@@
5. The priests’ first work is to teach the difference between holy and common, clean and unclean. In Christ, how is this teaching carried out today?
@@24518@@
The office of teaching
@@24519@@
Pastors and teachers have the responsibility to teach the congregation the truth of Scripture and discernment in daily life.
@@24520@@
The priesthood of all believers
@@24521@@
All believers take part in the witness of being set apart as holy (1 Peter 2:9).
@@24522@@
Showing it in life
@@24523@@
The most powerful teaching is to live out being set apart in life, not to press people down with words.
@@24524@@
The position of these notes: the core of teaching is not adding rules but giving “his difference” back to people. First show it by life, then explain it with words.
@@24525@@
Do not use “the difference between holy and common” to judge other people’s daily lives, or to put labels on people. Being set apart is for drawing nearer to him, not for drawing lines.

“I am their inheritance”: may those who serve go without security?
@@24527@@
6. The priests have no land, God is their inheritance, and they live on the people’s offerings. How should we understand the life and giving of those who serve today?
@@24528@@
Taking God as the inheritance
@@24529@@
The point is spiritual belonging: he is the greatest portion.
@@24530@@
The people’s responsibility
@@24531@@
The people are to supply the firstfruits, and the blessing also comes from this flow (44:30).
@@24532@@
Both go side by side
@@24533@@
Those who serve take God as their inheritance, and the congregation also has a responsibility to care for them fairly (1 Cor 9:13--14).
@@24534@@
The position of these notes: “I am their inheritance” is the greatest comfort for those who serve, and it does not free the congregation from the duty of fair support; the two go side by side and cannot replace each other.
@@24535@@
Do not use this verse to demand that those who serve “be content with poverty,” and do not use it to pressure believers to give. The weight of this verse is: God himself is the greatest portion.

Is the land map of chapter 45 literal, symbolic, or spiritual?
@@24537@@
1. Should the measurements and distribution of the holy portion, the city’s portion and the prince’s portion be understood literally, or as a picture of order?
@@24538@@
It believes there will be an actual distribution of land and a holy portion in the future.
@@24539@@
The map expresses an order: the center is God, then the priests, the Levites, the city and the prince, symbolizing God dwelling with his people.
@@24540@@
The map points to Christ and his people, and every portion is in Christ.
@@24541@@
These notes follow the issue “The New Temple: Is Chapters 40–48 Literal, Symbolic, or Spiritual?”: we set the views side by side, assume no timetable for building the temple, for sacrifices or for dividing the land, and do not judge brothers and sisters who see it differently. What the prophet wants us to see is: the center belongs to God, and power has limits.
@@24542@@
Please do not turn this question into a reason for a dispute over land, or use it to point at any modern place or people. Those who have known loss and displacement do not need to choose an explanation first before they can hear: he wants first to give the center to himself.

“The princes shall no more oppress my people”: where is the limit of power?
@@24544@@
2. The prince has land and offerings, yet he is asked to be content and to remove violence and robbery. Is this a rule for the kings of that time, or a principle for everyone who has authority?
@@24545@@
Aimed at the kings of that time
@@24546@@
It is a specific demand on the leaders of Israel, that they no longer oppress.
@@24547@@
A principle for all leaders
@@24548@@
Everyone who has authority has limits, and must answer for those entrusted to him.
@@24549@@
Pointing to the Messiah-King
@@24550@@
The king who truly does not oppress is Christ (Isa 11:3--5; Mark 10:45).
@@24551@@
The position of these notes: the three readings all lead to the same principle: power has limits, and the people entrusted to him are “my people.” Jesus is the true king, and we learn from him.
@@24552@@
Do not use this verse to blame a particular leader, or to demand that those who are oppressed “let go quickly.” Those who are oppressed are first seen, and only then do we talk about anything else.

Symbolic justice

The scales symbolize God’s justice and order.
